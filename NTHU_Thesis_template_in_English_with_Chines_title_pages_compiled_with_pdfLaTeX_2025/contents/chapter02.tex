\chapter{System Overview and Analysis Framework}

\section{Hexapole Electromagnetic Platform}

The platform considered in this dissertation is a Hall-sensor-based hexapole electromagnetic actuator for precision manipulation in aqueous solution. The actuator uses six independently driven poles to generate the magnetic field and magnetic-field gradient in a three-dimensional workspace. Because the force objective is three-dimensional while actuation is six-dimensional, the system is over-actuated and requires explicit allocation logic for practical control design.

From an engineering perspective, the platform combines high-force authority, non-contact operation, and compatibility with microscope-based experiments. These properties make it suitable for high-speed untethered manipulation, provided that magnetic-flux generation and multi-axis interaction are properly controlled.

\section{Sensing and Force-Production Chain}

The physical chain starts from actuation commands, proceeds through magnetic-flux generation at pole tips, and ends at force production on the magnetic probe. Hall-sensor measurements provide real-time information for closed-loop flux regulation, while force output is obtained through the flux-based force-production path.

For dissertation-level analysis, this chain is treated as a causal pipeline rather than independent modules. Upstream uncertainty in flux generation directly influences downstream force quality. Therefore, system identification, inner-loop flux control, and force-generation analysis are developed as connected threads.

\section{Layered Control Architecture}

The control architecture is organized into three layers:

\textbf{Layer 1: System identification.}
Open-loop calibration data are used to capture channel coupling, remanence/hysteresis-related effects, and dynamic behavior relevant to control design.

\textbf{Layer 2: Flux control.}
The inner loop regulates magnetic flux generation and addresses multi-axis interaction, disturbance effects, and bandwidth requirements for precision actuation.

\textbf{Layer 3: Force generation.}
Desired force behavior is rendered through allocation and scheduling decisions, and evaluated by main-axis quality and cross-axis interaction.

This layered architecture defines the technical backbone of this thesis and provides a direct map to Chapters 3 through 5.

\section{Shared Analysis and Validation Strategy}

To keep interpretation consistent across chapters, this dissertation uses a shared analysis strategy:

1. Start from observable physical behavior.
2. Convert observations into design-relevant model information.
3. Evaluate design choices by both simulation and experiment in the same thread.
4. Interpret outcomes by physical meaning and cross-layer impact.

This strategy is used to ensure that each reported performance change can be traced back to a specific design decision.

\section{Scope of Chapter 2}

Chapter 2 establishes the common language and system context for the rest of the dissertation. Detailed identification procedures are presented in Chapter 3, flux-control design and comparison in Chapter 4, and force-generation analysis with integrated validation in Chapter 5.
