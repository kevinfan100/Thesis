\chapter{Force Generation, Cross-Layer Validation, and Conclusions}

\section{Chapter Objective and Interface from Flux Control}

This chapter develops the force-generation stage using validated outputs from the flux-control stage. The objective is to evaluate how upstream identification and control decisions influence downstream force quality and operational behavior in a traceable manner.

\section{Force-Generation Interface and Allocation Logic}

This section defines the force-generation interface from desired task-level force behavior to actuator-level command variables. The emphasis is on interface consistency, allocation logic, and implementation assumptions required for reproducible operation.

\section{Force-Quality Criteria and Scheduling Context}

This section defines force-quality criteria used in this dissertation, including main-axis quality, cross-axis interaction behavior, and scheduling-related response characteristics. The criteria are selected to support control-oriented interpretation and chapter-to-chapter comparability.

\section{Simulation and Experimental Validation of Force Output}

This section presents simulation and experimental validation in the same technical thread to assess force-generation behavior under representative operating conditions. The goal is to verify whether force output quality is consistent with the intended design chain from identification and flux control.

\section{Cross-Layer Attribution: Identification to Flux to Force}

This section closes the evidence chain by attributing force-level outcomes to upstream decisions and validated interfaces. The discussion focuses on what is causally supported by evidence under tested conditions, and how this attribution supports reproducible development.

\section{Conclusions and Future Directions}

This section summarizes the dissertation-level contribution as an automation-oriented and control-grounded development framework for Hall-sensor-based hexapole magnetic tweezers. It also outlines practical next steps for extending the framework in future studies while preserving traceability and reproducibility.
